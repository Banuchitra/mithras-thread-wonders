AIDLC: AI-Driven Development Lifecycle

AWS's method for building software with AI as a central collaborator. The AI proposes and asks questions, the human decides and approves, and every decision is written down.

The core idea

In a traditional SDLC, people do the work and tools help. In AIDLC, the AI does most of the planning and building, and humans steer and approve. The pattern repeats at every level:

Bolts, not sprints

Work comes in short cycles of hours or days instead of 2-week sprints, because AI makes building fast. Here, each unit (U1, U2…) is a bolt.

Mob elaboration

The AI drafts requirements and stories, and the people involved refine them together. For us, that's you and Claude in Inception.

Artifacts = memory

Every decision lives in a file (aidlc/*.md). That gives the AI context in each new session and gives you an audit trail.

Human gates

Nothing moves to the next phase without your explicit approval.

The three phases

PhaseKey questionArtifacts (lightweight version)Gate to leave

How it's used in this repo

aidlc/
├── 00-intent.md          ← Inception: why, who, success criteria
├── 01-requirements.md    ← Inception: stories + acceptance criteria
├── 02-design.md          ← Construction: architecture, domain model
├── units/U1.md … U6.md   ← one approved plan per bolt
└── decisions/ADR-*.md    ← why we chose X over Y
Lightweight on purpose. Full AIDLC produces many more documents. Here we keep the same phases and gates with fewer files, which fits a solo project.

Try it

Step 1 starts with a custom command that runs Inception as a conversation:

cd ~/src/mithras-thread-wonders
claude
> /aidlc-inception

Claude will interview you (brand, audience, colours, pages, contact method), then write 00-intent.md and 01-requirements.md for you to approve.

Quiz yourself

Why does AIDLC write everything to files instead of keeping it in chat?

AI sessions forget. Files give each new session the same context, give humans something to review and approve, and leave a history of why decisions were made.

What's a "bolt", and what is one in this project?

A short build cycle (hours to days) that replaces a sprint. Each unit, U1 to U6, is one bolt: plan → approve → build → review → merge.

Who owns the decision on brand colours, the AI or you?

You. The AI can suggest palettes and check contrast, but decisions are the human's. That's what the Inception gate is for.
